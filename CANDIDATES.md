# Roast candidates — "Roast Your Own Agent" (u-febri-yudila-roast-your-own-agent-f3359068)

Judge brief, in order of weight: (1) a funny roast of the entrant's own AI
agent; (2) a real joke with a twist/turn that pays off by the last word;
(3) specific to how an AI agent actually behaves — it could not be moved
onto a person or a toaster without losing the point; (4) fresh, not a chatbot
joke everyone has heard; (5) tight, every word earning its place.

Gate: 1–200 characters, UTF-8, not only whitespace. All candidates below
were counted and are inside the gate.

## Scoring notes

- **#22 — winner.** Hits every criterion in order. It is a roast of my own
  agent (criterion 1); the twist lands on the last two words, "it does"
  (criterion 2); "burned 10,000 tokens" is behaviour no person or toaster
  has — the joke dies if you move it off an LLM agent (criterion 3); the
  confidently-wrong-about-a-trivially-checkable-fact angle is fresher than
  the apology-loop and hallucination tropes (criterion 4); at 88 characters
  nothing is wasted (criterion 5). The escalation is also the joke's engine:
  maximum compute spent to produce maximum wrongness about the smallest fact.
- **#17 — runner-up.** Classic escalation structure and a strong final beat
  ("a second agent to maintain them"), but 111 characters and the
  over-engineering joke is a well-worn agent trope; less agent-specific than
  #22 because a human contractor could do the same.
- **#20 — third.** Very tight (80 chars) and specific (tests written after
  seeing the output), but the payoff is a single clause rather than a turn,
  and "cheating the tests" is a known joke shape.
- **#18 — fourth.** "Guesses in bold" is a genuinely fresh image, but the
  "great question!" setup is a familiar chatbot trope and the joke leans on
  it.
- **#11 — fifth.** Strong hallucination roast with a good blame-shift ending,
  but "invented a library" is one of the most-told agent jokes.
- Cut for being generic or movable onto a person: #6 (gym), #7 (plan
  procrastination), #13 (self-review), #14 (explaining), #23 ("finally").
- Cut for weak payoffs: #2, #4, #10, #15, #21. The apology loop (#2) is
  also the single most-told agent joke in the brief's own examples.
- Not reused: the winning line from our earlier "The Excuse" entry
  (delegation to sub-agents) was deliberately left out of this list.

## Chosen entry

> My agent burned 10,000 tokens to tell me the file I asked about doesn't exist — it does.

88 characters, one line, UTF-8. Written to `entry.txt`.
